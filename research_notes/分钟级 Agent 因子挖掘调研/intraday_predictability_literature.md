# Intraday (minute-level) return predictability and ML models for intraday stock prediction

Scope note: covers the academic literature on what predicts returns from 1 minute to end of day and overnight, which model families work, and how studies set up labels and evaluation. The emphasis is on what can be done with 1-minute OHLCV bars (no order book). Research ran on 2026-11-02 with about 20 search/fetch calls. Most journal articles are paywalled, so many numbers below come from abstracts, working-paper versions, or repository pages. Each item says which. DOIs marked "(recalled, verify)" come from prior knowledge and were not confirmed in this session.

---

## Q1. What predicts intraday returns (intraday momentum, reversal, cross-sectional periodicity, overnight vs intraday split, seasonality)?

### Takeaway
At the market/index level, the best-documented minute-bar-computable predictor is **market intraday momentum**: the return from the previous close through the first half-hour, and the "rest-of-day" return, positively predict the **last half-hour** return. The out-of-sample R² is small (~1%) but economically meaningful. In the cross-section, the robust regularities are (a) **same-time-of-day periodicity**: a stock's return in a given half-hour continues at daily lags for about 40 days; (b) **very short-term reversal** (<1 hour) from bid-ask bounce and liquidity imbalance; (c) a strong **overnight vs intraday split**, where many anomaly profits fall entirely in one period with opposite signs in the other.

### Cited Findings
**Market intraday momentum (US)**
- Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market intraday momentum," *JFE* 129(2):394–414 (2018). S&P 500 ETF (SPY) high-frequency data, 1993–2013. The first half-hour return, measured from the previous day's close, predicts the last half-hour return. Predictability rises with volatility and volume, is stronger when the first half-hour return is positive, and survives "reasonable transaction costs." One summary reports out-of-sample R² ≈ 1.2% from the first-half-hour predictor alone (secondary; verify against the paper). Proposed mechanisms: infrequent rebalancing, and late-informed traders acting in the last half-hour. — [RePEc/EconPapers listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414); [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/); [working-paper PDF copy](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf); DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.009 (recalled, verify)
- Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum," *JFE* 142(1):377–403 (2021), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.04.029. 60+ futures (equity, bond, commodity, FX), 1974–2020. The last-30-minute return is positively predicted by the rest-of-day return in all asset classes, and the effect **reverts over the next days**. Mechanism: gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged-ETF rebalancing. On the S&P 500, momentum appears when net gamma exposure is negative and grows stronger as it becomes more negative. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403); [authors' PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Erasmus repository](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/)

**Cross-sectional intraday periodicity and reversal**
- Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka, "Intraday patterns in the cross-section of stock returns," *JF* 65(4):1369–1407 (2010). The day is split into 13 half-hour intervals. They find significant cross-sectional continuation of half-hour returns at lags that are **exact multiples of a trading day**, lasting about **40 trading days**. It is strongest in the first and last half-hours but not limited to them. They also find short-term **reversal** within the day, driven by liquidity imbalances lasting **less than an hour** and by bid-ask bounce. Firm size, systematic risk, and S&P 500 membership do not explain the pattern. Volume, order imbalance, volatility, and spreads show similar intraday patterns but do not explain the returns. They also note that timing trades with the pattern can cut execution costs by about the effective spread. — [arXiv 1005.3535](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3535); [EconPapers JF listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:65:y:2010:i:4:p:1369-1407); [UH PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/Heston_Korajczyk_Sadka_paper_UH.pdf)

**Overnight vs intraday decomposition**
- Lou, Polk & Skouras, "A tug of war: Overnight versus intraday expected returns," *JFE* 134(1):192–213 (2019). Across 14 trading strategies (momentum, value, size, etc.), profits tend to come **entirely from either the overnight or the intraday period**, and the other component usually has the opposite sign. Institutions trade more during the day and against momentum. Individuals dominate overnight. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:134:y:2019:i:1:p:192-213); [LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf); [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)

**High-frequency (seconds to minutes) predictability decay**
- Aït-Sahalia, Fan, Xue & Zhou, "How and when are high-frequency stock returns predictable?" NBER WP 30366 (Aug 2022). Predictability of high-frequency returns and durations is "large, systematic and pervasive," unlike low-frequency returns. Secondary coverage reports about 63–68% direction accuracy over the next few seconds, rising to 79% in a simulated "peek" at incoming order flow. It also reports ~80% of predictability coming from the most recent ~10 ms / 10 transactions, and prediction "no better than a coin toss" by about **5 minutes** (~2,000 transactions). Predictability is higher for lower-priced, less liquid stocks and near the end of the day. These numbers come from press coverage, not the paper's tables, so verify them. — [NBER w30366](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30366); [NBER PDF](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30366/w30366.pdf); [IR Impact coverage](https://www.ir-impact.com/2022/08/movement-lower-priced-stocks-easier-predict-hft-firms-finds-princeton-paper)

**Intraday-derived signals for longer horizons**
- Amaya, Christoffersen, Jacobs & Vasquez (*JFE* 2015) build weekly realized moments from intraday data. Long lowest-realized-skewness decile minus short highest decile earns about **19 bp the following week**, not explained by FF/Carhart factors. — [SUFE academic newsletter summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356501)

### Inferences
- Pure 1-min OHLCV data is enough to compute every predictor above: first-half-hour, rest-of-day, same-slot lagged returns, overnight gap, realized moments. Order-book data is not needed for these daily/half-hour-scale effects.
- The literature points to three horizon classes with different economics. (i) Seconds to a few minutes: high predictability, mostly microstructure and order-flow driven, gone within ~5 min, unlikely to be capturable from 1-min bars after costs. (ii) Half-hour to end-of-day: modest R² (~1–4%) with time-of-day-specific structure (last half-hour). (iii) Overnight vs intraday splits of daily returns, where clientele effects create strong sign differences.
- A minute-level agent should expose **time-of-day-anchored targets** (e.g., return to close, last-30-min return, next-day open gap), not only rolling "next-N-minute" targets, since the documented effects are tied to the clock.

### Gaps
- Exact out-of-sample R² and after-cost Sharpe for Gao et al. (2018) were not confirmed from the published text.
- The R²-by-horizon table for Aït-Sahalia et al. was not accessed. The seconds/5-minute figures are from press coverage.
- No study found that systematically measures predictability decay across 1, 5, 15, 30, 60 min and EOD on the same 1-min-bar dataset.

## Q2. ML for high-frequency/intraday prediction: which model families work, at what horizon, with what accuracy/IC?

### Takeaway
On order books, CNN-LSTM models such as **DeepLOB** reach 75–84% three-class accuracy on the FI-2010 benchmark at short event horizons. But an independent re-benchmark (**LOBCAST**) found that **all 15 LOB models lose much of that performance on new data**, and accuracy falls as the horizon lengthens. Training on **order-flow** inputs instead of raw book states, and pooling across stocks ("universal" models), work better than per-stock models. On bar data, **tree ensembles on lagged cross-sectional returns** have produced after-cost Sharpe ≈1 at 5-minute frequency. The **Kronos** K-line foundation model reports small but positive IC (~0.04–0.07) on minute-to-hourly bars. The honest effect size at bar frequency is IC on the order of a few percent.

### Cited Findings
**Order-book deep learning**
- Zhang, Zohren & Roberts, "DeepLOB: Deep convolutional neural networks for limit order books" (arXiv 1808.03668; published *IEEE Trans. Signal Processing* 67(11), 2019, DOI 10.1109/TSP.2019.2907260, recalled, verify). It uses a CNN plus Inception plus LSTM on raw 10-level LOB. On FI-2010, Setup 1 at horizon k=100 events gives **75.01% accuracy / 74.96% F1**. Setup 2 at k=10 gives **84.47% accuracy / 83.40% F1**. A later paper's reproduction lists k=20 at 74.85% and k=50 at 80.51%. Performance on stationary labels deteriorates as the horizon grows. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.03668); [Multi-horizon LOB forecasting, arXiv 2105.10430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10430)
- Prata et al., "LOB-based deep learning models for stock price trend prediction: a benchmark study" (arXiv 2308.01915, LOBCAST framework). They re-run 15 SOTA LOB models. Reproduced FI-2010 results **differ from the originally reported numbers**, and **all models show a significant performance drop on new data**, which raises doubts about real-world use. — [arXiv 2308.01915](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01915); [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.01915)
- Sirignano & Cont, "Universal features of price formation in financial markets: perspectives from deep learning," *Quantitative Finance* 19(9):1449–1459 (2019), DOI 10.1080/14697688.2019.1622295. An LSTM is trained on high-frequency order-book data for hundreds of US stocks. A **universal model pooled across all stocks beats stock-specific** linear and nonlinear models out of sample, including on stocks not in training. Longer price and order-flow history improves accuracy, which suggests path-dependence. Accuracy is stable over time. — [arXiv 1803.06917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06917); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:19:y:2019:i:9:p:1449-1459)
- Kolm, Turiel & Westray, "Deep order flow imbalance: Extracting alpha at multiple horizons from the limit order book," *Mathematical Finance* 33(4):1044–1081 (2023). 115 Nasdaq stocks, multi-horizon return forecasts. Simple off-the-shelf networks trained on **order-flow (OFI) inputs** beat most models trained on raw order-book states. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:mathfi:v:33:y:2023:i:4:p:1044-1081)
- LOBench (Zhong, Lin, Yang 2025, arXiv 2505.02139): curated **China A-share LOB** datasets and a benchmark for representation learning (reconstruction, prediction, imputation). — [arXiv 2505.02139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02139)
- Transformers for LOB: TransLOB (arXiv 2003.00130) and TLOB, a dual-attention transformer (arXiv 2502.15757). — [TransLOB](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00130); [TLOB](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15757v2)

**Bar-based ML (no order book)**
- Huddleston, Liu & Stentoft, "Intraday market predictability: A machine learning approach," *J. Financial Econometrics* 21(2):485–527 (2023). **5-minute** market-index return predicted from **lagged returns of index constituents**. Nonlinear tree-based models show significant predictability, ensembles do best, and the strategy reaches **Sharpe 0.98 after transaction costs**. Per Huddleston's dissertation, predictability fell after decimalization, was higher midday and in high-volatility or illiquid periods, and is linked to slow-moving capital. Follow-up "Intraday stock predictability everywhere" (Liu & Stentoft, 2023 WP) extends this to ETFs, sectors, and single S&P 500 stocks. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jfinec:v:21:y:2023:i:2:p:485-527.); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v21y2023i2p485-527..html); [UWO dissertation](https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/etd/8292); [CXO summary of follow-up](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/robustness-and-exploitability-of-intraday-stock-return-prediction/)
- **Kronos** (Shi, Fu et al., arXiv 2508.02739; AAAI 2026). A decoder-only foundation model for K-lines (OHLCV + amount). A Transformer tokenizer (Binary Spherical Quantization) turns each bar into hierarchical discrete tokens, then the model is pre-trained autoregressively on **>12B K-line records from 45 exchanges** across 7 frequencies (1-min to weekly). Sizes: small 24.7M, base 102.3M, large 499.2M parameters, with max context 512. Forecast evaluation covers 5/10/15/20/40-min, 1/2/4-h, and daily bars. For 5-min bars the lookback is 480 and the horizon 96; for daily, lookback 40 and horizon 12. The test period starts July 2024 (post-cutoff) on XSHG, NASDAQ, JPX, NSE, KRX, HKEX, plus out-of-distribution markets. Headline claims: price-forecast RankIC **+93% vs the best time-series foundation model** and +87% vs the best non-pretrained baseline, and 9% lower volatility-forecast MAE. Absolute numbers in the ablation (Kronos-small): **price IC 0.043 / RankIC 0.025; return IC 0.067 / RankIC 0.062**; volatility R² 0.249. The investment simulation is **daily, not intraday**: CSI 300/800 via Qlib, top-k long, ≥5-day holding, 0.15% cost per trade. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02739); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739v1); [AAAI OJS PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/39730/43691)
- A 2019 preprint (Yang et al., per NEP-MST listing) reports CNN and LSTM on high-frequency price-volume data for Chinese stocks, with annualized net returns of 62% (CNN) and 50% (LSTM) "despite considerable fees." Peer-review status unverified and the numbers are implausibly high. Treat with skepticism. — [NEP-MST 2019-11-11](https://nep.repec.org/nep-mst/2019-11-11)

### Inferences
- For a 1-min OHLCV agent, the most transferable lessons are: (1) **pool across stocks** (universal model, per Sirignano & Cont); (2) use **cross-sectional lagged-return features** (per Huddleston et al.); (3) expect ICs of roughly 0.03–0.07, not 70–80% accuracy. The FI-2010-style accuracies are on mid-price direction at event horizons with LOB inputs and stationary (smoothed) labels, and they do not carry over to bar data or real trading.
- Expected ranking of model families for bar data: GBDT on hand-crafted features is a strong, cheap baseline. Sequence models (GRU/LSTM/Transformer) and pre-trained models like Kronos are candidates to add, not replacements. The LOBCAST result argues for strict out-of-period, out-of-universe tests before trusting any deep model.
- Kronos is directly usable on A-share minute K-lines (OHLCV + amount) and could serve as a feature extractor or baseline forecaster for 5–60-min horizons. Its reported economic test is daily, so its intraday economic value is unproven.

### Gaps
- Full Kronos-vs-baseline tables per frequency (Appendix F) were not read, so IC by frequency and horizon is unknown.
- No exact horizons or IC numbers were retrieved for Kolm et al. (2023).
- I found no peer-reviewed study that applies CNN/LSTM/Transformer to **1-min OHLCV bars of CSI 300 constituents** with realistic costs. The A-share minute-bar ML literature found was mostly preprints or broker research.

## Q3. China A-share evidence: T+1, overnight/intraday, intraday momentum/reversal, minute-level factors

### Takeaway
A-shares differ from the US in ways that matter for target design. **Average overnight returns are negative** and intraday returns positive, which papers attribute largely to **T+1**: shares bought today cannot be sold until tomorrow, so buyers pay a premium intraday and sell at the open. There is **significant intraday momentum**, but the predictive power sits more in the **overnight component** and the **second-to-last (14:00–14:30) half-hour**, and less in the opening half-hour. There are **cross-sectional reversals of intraday returns**, and at daily/weekly frequency **contrarian effects dominate momentum**, also linked to T+1.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang, Ma & Zhu (2019), "Intraday momentum and stock return predictability: Evidence from China," *Economic Modelling* 76:319–329. High-frequency Chinese market index data. The **first and/or the seventh (second-to-last) half-hour returns** significantly predict the **last half-hour** return in and out of sample, with economic gains for market timing and asset allocation. The 90-minute lunch break lets morning returns predict afternoon returns. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ecmode:v:76:y:2019:i:c:p:319-329)
- Firm-level Chinese intraday momentum (Hull repository paper, revised 2019, also cited as Gao et al. 2019): there is strong firm-level intraday momentum, but predictability comes **mainly from the overnight component, not the opening half-hour**. The authors link this to the pre-open call auction. — [Hull repository](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/3016448); [related Jin et al. record](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/output/3295550/intraday-time-series-momentum-evidence-from-china)
- Lu Yang (Shenzhen University): the 7th and 8th half-hour returns predict later returns. For CSI 300, R² ≈ **3.8%** for the last half-hour based on the 7th half-hour, which is stronger than the first-half-hour effect, unlike the US. (Snippet-level; full text not verified.) — [SJTU ACEM PDF](https://www.acem.sjtu.edu.cn/ueditor/jsp/upload/file/20230705/1688558855821058862.pdf)
- Chu, Gu & Zhou (2019), *Finance Research Letters* 30:83–88: with the first-half-hour return as predictor, there is **significant intraday momentum and a reversal effect** in Chinese stocks, attributed to noise trading. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finlet:v:30:y:2019:i:c:p:83-88); [CSU faculty profile](https://facultyprofile.csuohio.edu/en/publications/intraday-momentum-and-reversal-in-chinese-stock-market-7/)
- Chu & Song (2023), "Cross-sectional reversal of intraday returns and investor heterogeneity in an emerging market," *Borsa Istanbul Review* 23(3):614–627. Confirms **cross-sectional reversals of intraday returns** in A-shares, robust to seasonality, alternative samples, and the daily price-limit rule. — [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/cross-sectional-reversal-of-intraday-returns-and-investor-heterogeneity-in-an-emerging-market-chu-xiaojun/10014308779); [DOAJ record](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/doaj.art-055e923724bf457aa21b64ec9c9f967a)
- Limkriangkrai, Chai & Zheng (2023), *Pacific-Basin Finance Journal* 80: in APAC ETFs, intraday momentum is mainly evident in **China and Japan**, weaker or absent in Korea, Hong Kong, and Singapore, and weaker during COVID-19. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v80y2023ics0927538x2300152x.html)
- T+1 and contrarian effects: a 2024 *Emerging Markets Finance and Trade* paper (60(10):2237–2260) finds significant **contrarian effects at daily, weekly, and monthly** frequencies and insignificant momentum, attributing this to T+1. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/mes/emfitr/v60y2024i10p2237-2260.html)
- Qiao & Dam (2020), per search-engine summary: the average Chinese overnight return is **negative**, and the T+1 rule contributes significantly to this "overnight return puzzle." (Primary source not retrieved.) Yin & Ma (2021), *Systems Engineering – Theory & Practice*, frame "positive overnight, negative daytime reversal" as a tug of war between heterogeneous investors. — [SETP record](https://sysengi.cjoe.ac.cn/EN/10.12011/SETP2020-1958); [Frontiers energy-stocks overnight/intraday mispricing paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2021.807881/full)
- Xie, Xia & Gao (2025, *Research in International Business and Finance* 78): overnight anomalies in A-shares can persist for 1, 3, 6, and 12 months. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/riibaf/v78y2025ics0275531925002752.html)
- Leippold, Wang & Zhou, "Machine learning in the Chinese stock market," *JFE* (2022). Monthly horizon, not intraday. **Liquidity is the most important predictor** in China, retail-driven small-cap predictability is strong, and long-short remains significant after costs. — [SFI publication page](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/machine-learning-in-the-chinese-stock-market)
- Minute/5-min-derived factors in China: 5-min realized jump measures (sign realized jump) relate to the cross-section of returns (PLOS ONE 2017). 5-min realized semi-coskewness aggregated weekly predicts returns. — [PMC5542663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5542663/); [Dryad dataset](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.80gb5mkx7)

### Inferences
- Under T+1, **a long-only A-share position opened intraday cannot be closed the same day**. So "next 5/15/30-minute return" targets are only economically tradable for (a) entry/exit timing of positions that are held anyway (execution alpha), (b) T+0 rotation against an existing inventory ("底仓 T+0"), or (c) instruments that allow T+0 (index futures, some ETFs). This is an inference from the T+1 rule and is not tested in the cited papers.
- The natural A-share targets suggested by this literature are: **open→close (intraday)**, **close→next open (overnight)**, **entry-time→next-day close/VWAP** (the minimum holding period under T+1), **last-30-min return** (market timing), and **cross-sectional intraday return rank** (reversal). These match where documented effects exist.
- The overnight leg is mechanically negative on average in A-shares. Cross-sectional ranking of overnight returns, or market-neutralization, is needed to avoid learning the mean.
- The lunch break (11:30–13:00) and opening/closing call auctions split the 240 1-min bars into sessions. Session-aware features and labels (morning→afternoon) are justified by Zhang, Ma & Zhu (2019).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve specific Chinese-journal (金融研究, 管理科学学报) minute-factor papers in this session. The searches mostly returned English-language work, so a targeted Chinese-language search (e.g., CNKI) is still needed.
- Qiao & Dam (2020) primary text not located. Exact magnitudes of the A-share negative overnight return were not confirmed.
- There is no direct study of how T+1 changes the **tradability** of minute-horizon ML signals in A-shares.

## Q4. Transaction-cost realism at minute horizons

### Takeaway
Minute-horizon predictability must clear a fixed A-share cost floor. **Stamp duty is 5 bp, sell side only, since 28 Aug 2023** (down from 10 bp). On top come commissions, transfer fees, half-spread, and impact, so a round trip plausibly costs roughly **10–20+ bp** for liquid large caps. Seconds-level predictability vanishes within ~5 minutes, and Gao et al. and Huddleston et al. survive costs only at half-hour or 5-minute frequency with tree-ensemble signals. So per-trade expected edge, not IC, decides tradability.

### Cited Findings
- A-share stamp duty was halved from 0.1% to **0.05%**, collected only on the seller, effective 28 Aug 2023. It also applies to Stock Connect northbound trades. Previous change: 19 Sep 2008. At least 30 brokers cut commissions at the same time. — [gov.cn (State Council English)](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/policywatch/202308/28/content_WS64ec5513c6d0868f4e8dee23.html); [HKEX circular](https://www.hkex.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/Services/Circulars-and-Notices/Participant-and-Members-Circulars/SEHK/2023/CT13723B.pdf); [21jingji/CBN](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20230828/herald/faab376b468f1b96cbaa8baac81c1c42.html)
- Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010): timing trades with the intraday pattern can save about the **effective spread**, i.e., the half-hour periodicity is about the same size as the spread. — [arXiv 1005.3535](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3535)
- Gao et al. (2018): market intraday momentum persists after "reasonable transaction costs" (for SPY, an ultra-liquid ETF). — [WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)
- Huddleston, Liu & Stentoft (2023): 5-min ensemble strategy **Sharpe 0.98 after transaction costs**. Predictability is higher in illiquid periods, where costs are also higher. — [SearchWorks abstract](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/edb__162786361); [UWO](https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/etd/8292)
- Aït-Sahalia et al. (2022): high predictability exists at the second/transaction level but is "no better than a coin toss" by ~5 minutes (press coverage). This means the strongest predictability is at horizons where only very low-latency, low-cost traders can capture it. — [IR Impact](https://www.ir-impact.com/2022/08/movement-lower-priced-stocks-easier-predict-hft-firms-finds-princeton-paper); [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30366)
- Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz ("Trading Costs," AQR live-trade data): actual institutional costs are much smaller than earlier academic estimates. Size, value, and momentum survive costs. **Short-term reversal is the exception on capacity**, being the most cost-sensitive. Costs rise with volatility. — [NBER draft PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f68262/f68262.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/surprise-the-size-value-and-momentum-anomalies-survive-after-trading-costs/)
- Kronos's A-share backtest assumes **0.15% per trade** with a ≥5-day holding period. This shows the cost level researchers assume for A-share daily strategies. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739v1)

### Inferences
- Rough break-even under T+1, for illustration (my calculation, not from a source): with ~5 bp stamp duty, ~1–2.5 bp commission per side, ~1–5 bp half-spread per side for CSI 300 names, and some impact, a round trip costs about 10–20 bp. A signal must deliver more than that **per trade in expected return**. With intraday return vol of ~100–200 bp per day for a typical stock, an IC of ~0.05 gives an expected edge of about IC × σ × (z-score of selection), roughly a few to ~10 bp for top-decile picks. That is marginal, which is consistent with minute-level signals being mostly useful for **execution timing and for improving daily signals**, not standalone round trips.
- The agent should report the **cost-adjusted** metrics directly: expected bp per trade, turnover, break-even cost. Reporting only IC/accuracy is not enough.
- Short-term reversal signals, which are among the strongest at intraday horizons, are the most cost-fragile according to FIM and HKS.

## Q5. Label design and sampling (fixed horizon, triple-barrier, vol-scaled, cross-sectional rank; time vs volume bars) and evaluation

### Takeaway
The literature uses four label families. (1) **Fixed-horizon mid-price direction with a threshold or smoothing** (DeepLOB/FI-2010, 3-class up/stationary/down over k events). (2) **Fixed clock-time returns anchored to session points** (first/last half-hour, open→close, close→open). (3) **Cross-sectional returns and ranks evaluated by IC/RankIC** (Kronos, Qlib-style, Leippold et al.). (4) **Path-dependent triple-barrier labels**, optionally with meta-labeling, evaluated with **purged K-fold and an embargo** (López de Prado). Volume/dollar bars have statistical appeal, but evidence that they improve strategy performance is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- DeepLOB/FI-2010 labels: direction of the smoothed mid-price over the next k ∈ {10, 20, 50, 100} events with a threshold for "stationary". Accuracy drops as k grows. LOBCAST notes non-reproducibility and poor generalization of such setups. — [DeepLOB arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.03668); [LOBCAST arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01915)
- Kronos evaluates **IC and RankIC** of price and return forecasts (cross-sectional) at fixed bar horizons, plus MAE/R² for realized volatility. Its trading signal is the mean of 10 forecasted closes vs the current close. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02739v1)
- Heston et al. and Gao et al.: half-hour time-of-day buckets, with labels such as the last-half-hour return and same-slot next-day return. — [arXiv 1005.3535](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3535); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)
- Triple-barrier: the label is whichever barrier is hit first: upper (take-profit), lower (stop-loss), or vertical (time). Meta-labeling sets label = 1 when the primary model's side matches the realized outcome. Barrier widths are typically scaled by recent volatility. The labels pair with **purged K-fold CV**, which removes training samples whose label windows overlap the test set, plus an **embargo** after each test fold. Practitioner caution: barrier thresholds below round-trip costs produce labels that capture cost-level noise. (Secondary documentation; primary is López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, Wiley 2018, chs. 3–7.) — [finmlkit docs](https://finmlkit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/finmlkit.label.kit.html); [AFML summary note](https://qgtm-docs.pages.dev/research/gold_silver/paper_lopez_de_prado_afml); [MQL5 bars article](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/23310)
- Volume/dollar/run bars: AFML argues they have better statistical properties (closer to i.i.d. and normal returns, sampling by information arrival). One empirical practitioner test found these statistical advantages **do not reliably carry through to strategy performance**. — [MQL5 article](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/23310)
- Sirignano & Cont: pooled training across stocks with out-of-sample tests on **unseen stocks and later periods** is the strongest generalization test. — [arXiv 1803.06917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06917)

### Inferences
- Recommended label menu for a 1-min OHLCV agent, synthesized from the above:
  1. **Fixed-horizon forward log return** at h ∈ {1, 5, 15, 30, 60} min, **truncated at session end**. Do not cross lunch or the overnight gap unless that is intended.
  2. **Session-anchored** targets: t→11:30, t→15:00 close, last-30-min return, close→next open, and t→next-day close (the T+1 minimum hold).
  3. **Volatility-scaled** versions (return / trailing realized vol) to make the targets comparable across stocks and times of day, given the intraday U-shaped volatility.
  4. **Cross-sectional demeaned or rank** targets per timestamp, evaluated by RankIC per bar then averaged, mirroring Kronos and Qlib practice.
  5. Optionally **triple-barrier** with vol-scaled barriers set **wider than round-trip cost** and a vertical barrier at session end.
- Evaluation should use purged walk-forward splits with an embargo of at least the label horizon. Labels at 1-min granularity overlap heavily, so naive k-fold leaks. Report metrics by time-of-day bucket, since effects are clock-dependent (Heston et al.; Gao et al.).
- Time bars are the pragmatic default given the 1-min OHLCV input. Volume-clock resampling (e.g., equal-volume bars built from 1-min bars) is cheap to offer as an option, but the evidence that it helps is weak.
